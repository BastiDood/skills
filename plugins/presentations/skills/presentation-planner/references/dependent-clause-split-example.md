# Dependent Clause Split

<original_verbatim_text>

The general theme is that skill tradeoffs must be made. If we are to embrace these AI tools and fully extract their value in the workplace, then we must be prepared to let go of incidental skills that hinder the work our role values.

</original_verbatim_text>

<expected_on_slide_content>

- Embrace the **AI tools**
- Let go of incidental skills
- Protect the work that matters

</expected_on_slide_content>

<expected_presenter_notes>

- The general theme is that skill tradeoffs must be made.
  - If we are to embrace these AI tools and fully extract their value in the workplace,
    - then we must be prepared to let go of incidental skills that hinder the work our role values.

</expected_presenter_notes>

<anti_example>

- The general theme is that skill tradeoffs must be made.
  - If we are to embrace these AI tools and fully extract their value in the workplace,
    - then we must be prepared to let go of incidental skills that hinder the work our role values.

</anti_example>

<why_the_anti_example_fails>

This hierarchy is correct for presenter notes but exhausting as visible copy. The slide should compress the condition and consequence into parallel cues while the notes preserve the exact dependent-clause structure.

</why_the_anti_example_fails>

<rule_demonstrated>

Nest the supporting sentence in presenter notes, then split its condition and consequence at the comma. Keep the comma with the condition and preserve lowercase `then` in the level-2 continuation; do not mirror that scripted hierarchy onto the slide.

</rule_demonstrated>
