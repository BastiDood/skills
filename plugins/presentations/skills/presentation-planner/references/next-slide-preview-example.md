# Next-Slide Preview and Delivery Cue

<original_verbatim_text>

Compaction preserves the gist. But what does it lose? The details disappear. This changes how we should manage context.

</original_verbatim_text>

<interaction_requirement>

Advance after the question and let the audience read the comparison before continuing.

</interaction_requirement>

<expected_on_slide_content>

Slide 1:

- **Compaction** preserves the gist

Slide 2:

- What gets **lost**?
- The details

Slide 3:

- Manage **context** differently

</expected_on_slide_content>

<expected_presenter_notes>

Slide 1:

- Compaction preserves the gist.
- (But what does it lose? …)

Slide 2:

- But what does it lose?
  - The details disappear.
- [Read slide.]
- (This changes how we should manage context…)

Slide 3:

- This changes how we should manage context.

</expected_presenter_notes>

<anti_example>

Slide 1 on-slide content:

- Compaction preserves the gist.
- (But what does it lose? …)

Slide 1 presenter notes:

- Compaction preserves the gist.
- (But what does it lose? …)
- Presenter-note word count includes the preview.

</anti_example>

<why_the_anti_example_fails>

It exposes the private handoff cue to the audience and counts it as spoken source. The preview belongs only at the end of the outgoing presenter notes and only the incoming slide owns its source coverage.

</why_the_anti_example_fails>

<rule_demonstrated>

Copy the next slide's first spoken bullet or clause into the outgoing notes, then mark it with parentheses and an ellipsis. Exclude the preview from outgoing counts and coverage. `[Read slide.]` is a separate non-spoken cue; the final slide has no preview.

</rule_demonstrated>
